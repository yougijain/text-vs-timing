Result

Does when a post goes up add anything over what it says?

Not measurably.

The largest change from adding the posting time, across the 4 models, was for BERT: accuracy went from 60.5% to 59.7%, a change of -0.8 points on 3999 held-out posts.

Post by post, for TF-IDF, the timestamp corrected 34 posts the text-only model got wrong and broke 32 it had got right (p = 0.902, chi-square with continuity correction).

BERT and TF-IDF are within sampling error of each other (0.6052 vs 0.6104). The transformer is not earning its compute on this data, which is worth knowing before anyone deploys one.

An inconclusive result is a result. It means that on this corpus the posting time carries no usable signal once the text is accounted for, which is worth more than a tuned number that happened to go up.

What this cannot tell you

Every temporal feature here is UTC, and that bounds what the result means. "Late night" is the 00:00–05:00 UTC band, not the small hours where the poster was sitting. Without a per-author timezone, a local clock cannot be recovered: someone in California writing at 2am local lands at 09:00 UTC and is not flagged; someone in Berlin writing at 2am local is.

The feature is still predictive, because UTC hour correlates with local hour and with how many people are awake to vote on a post. It is not evidence about anyone's sleep, and nothing here should be read as a claim about a person.
